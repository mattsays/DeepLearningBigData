Limitations of Conventional Neural Architectures
MLPs, CNNs and Transformers are designed around vectors, grids and sequences

A forward pass transforms inputs into predictions through layers of weighted connections. During training, backpropagation computes gradients to update the weights and reduce prediction error.

Temporal dynamics
Frame-based processing computes even without new events, loses exact event timing and wastes cost on sparse, asynchronous signals.
\(\displaystyle \tau\frac{dU(t)}{dt} = -(U(t)-U_{rest}) + R\,I(t)\)
\(U\): membrane potential, \(U_{rest}\): resting potential, \(I\): input current, \(R\): membrane resistance, \(\tau\): time constant (anticipates LIF).
Neurons keep an internal state that evolves over time.
Relational structure
Social networks, molecules, roads, knowledge graphs: entities plus relations that standard models may not exploit explicitly.
\(\displaystyle f(PX,\,PAP^{\top}) = P\,f(X,A)\)
\(X\): node features, \(A\): adjacency matrix, \(P\): permutation matrix, \(f\): graph function. Reordering nodes reorders outputs accordingly.
Permutation equivariance: a core property of GNNs.
