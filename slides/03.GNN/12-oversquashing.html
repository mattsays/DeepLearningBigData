Limitation: Over-Squashing & Long-Range Dilemma
Why high-order feature fusion fails: exponential K-hop messages crushed into fixed-size vectors
1. Exponential K-Hop Growth
To reach distant nodes, receptive fields expand exponentially with layers \(K\) (\(1 \to 2 \to 4 \to 8\)).
→
2. Topological Bottleneck
Distant subgraphs must funnel massive traffic through narrow structural bridges (red choke point).
→
3. Fixed-Vector Compression
↓ squash ↓
Distinct node signals get crushed into one fixed \(d\)-dimensional embedding, erasing fine details.
Information Bottleneck & Signal Loss
While \(K\)-hop volume scales as \(\mathcal{O}(b^K)\), node memory stays fixed at \(\mathbb{R}^d\). Distant messages suffer severe lossy compression and vanishing Jacobian sensitivity.
High-Order Fusion Trade off
Stacking deeper layers (\(K \uparrow\)) for long-range reach triggers a double trap: Over-squashing (choked distant signals) and Over-smoothing (all nodes converge to identical values).
\(\displaystyle \underbrace{|\mathcal{N}_K(v)| \sim \mathcal{O}(b^{K})}_{\text{Exponential receptive field}} \;\gg\; \underbrace{d}_{\text{Fixed vector}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left\| \frac{\partial h_v^{(K)}}{\partial h_u^{(0)}} \right\| \xrightarrow{K \to \infty} 0 \;\;\text{(Lost long-range dependency)}\)
