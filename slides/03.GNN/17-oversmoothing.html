Limitation: Over-Smoothing & Feature Collapse
Why stacking deep message-passing layers erases node identity across the graph
1. Shallow (Layer 0)
Feature diversity (Dirichlet energy): High
Nodes preserve unique local features \(h_v^{(0)}\), but lack long-range context.
→
2. Moderate (Layer 2–4)
Feature diversity: Medium (Local Mixing)
Repeated neighborhood averaging acts as a low-pass filter, blending features.
→
3. Deep Stack (Layer \(K \gg 1\))
Feature diversity: Collapsed (\(\to 0\))
Receptive fields overlap globally: all node embeddings become indistinguishable.
Spectral Cause: Laplacian Smoothing
Each message-passing layer averages a node with its neighbors \(\mathcal{N}(v)\). Stacking \(K\) layers repeatedly applies a low-pass graph filter, damping high-frequency node differences.
The “Short Blanket” Depth Dilemma
We cannot simply increase depth \(K\) for high-order feature fusion: few layers miss distant nodes, while deep stacks erase local node identity and ruin node-level tasks.
\(\displaystyle \lim_{K \to \infty} \mathcal{E}_{\text{Dirichlet}}\!\left(H^{(K)}\right) = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \left\| h_u^{(K)} - h_v^{(K)} \right\|_2^2 \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h_v^{(K)} \approx h_u^{(K)} \;\;\forall\, u, v \in V\)
