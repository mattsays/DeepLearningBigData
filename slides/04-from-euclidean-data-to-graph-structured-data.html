From Euclidean Data to Graph-Structured Data
Why do we need Graph Neural Networks?
CNNs · regular grids
Designed for regular, grid-like data; each element has a fixed spatial position.
Example: images as 2D pixel grids.
RNNs · sequences
Designed for sequential data; elements follow an ordered sequence.
Examples: text, time series.
GNNs · arbitrary graphs
Non-Euclidean data: nodes are entities, edges are relationships; any connectivity, variable size.
Examples: social networks, molecules, citation networks.
Traditional architectures
Graph Neural Networks
Fixed, regular structure
Arbitrary topology
Spatial or sequential relationships
Explicit relational connections
Natural ordering of elements
No canonical node ordering
GNNs extend deep learning to data whose structure is defined by relationships rather than by a regular grid or sequence.
